# lptq-block-ap
Addresses: error accumulates block by block; a layer-wise solver cannot move continuous weights to absorb the rounding error of other layers in the block; at two to three bits, rounding-only freedom is small. Measured as: per block, the block-output MSE against the full-precision block on calibration inputs after a layer-wise solve.
Mechanism: Block-wise quantization-aware training. For each decoder block in sequence, train all block weights, step sizes and zero points through a straight-through rounding estimator. The loss is the block-output MSE against the full-precision block. After a block is trained, round its weights and freeze them; the next block trains on that quantized block's outputs.
Effect: per block, the block-output MSE on the quantized-upstream inputs is minimised jointly over weights, step sizes and zero points; the weights move away from the originals.
Preconditions: one block plus its optimizer state fits on a card; per-block calibration inputs from the quantized upstream can be cached or recomputed; the target format is a grouped integer scheme with per-group scale (and zero point).
Inference overhead: none (standard grouped int).
Pitfalls: the block loss is local and does not see the model output; a block can reach a lower block MSE by moving weights in a direction the next block may then partly undo. Second: with the straight-through estimator, the trainer forward and the exported integer forward differ; if the export does not reproduce the trainer bit for bit, the per-block numbers describe the trainer model, not the served model.
Origin: Chen et al. (EfficientQAT) observed that at low bit widths a layer-wise grid leaves the block output off its full-precision target. Step-size training from nearest rounding cannot close this gap, so they train weights, step sizes and zero points per block.
