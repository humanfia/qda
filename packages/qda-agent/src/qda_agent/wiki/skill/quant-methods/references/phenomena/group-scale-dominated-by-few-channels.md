# group-scale-dominated-by-few-channels
Observable: inside a quantization group of a weight matrix's input columns, the ratio of the group's largest absolute value to its root-mean-square is above the ratio that a group of that width gives for values of similar size. The same columns are the loud ones in every group of the layer. The same channels are loud across calibration texts.
How to see it on your own model: for one Linear, reshape its input dimension into groups of the container's group size and histogram absmax/rms per group. Separately, histogram the per-channel mean of |x| at that Linear's input on any calibration batch. Both need one forward hook and no build. The phenomenon is present when a small fraction of channels sits orders of magnitude above the rest and that set is stable between batches. A group with a high absmax/rms gets a coarser step for its other columns than a group with the same rms and no outlier. So its bound on the round-to-nearest weight error is larger. A model can show it strongly while its layer-output error stays small, because the loud channels may be ones the layer's output barely depends on. Pair it with the input-weighted version (weight each channel by its mean square input) before concluding anything.
