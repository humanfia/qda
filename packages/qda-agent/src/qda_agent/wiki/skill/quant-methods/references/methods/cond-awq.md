# cond-awq
Addresses: a few input channels with large activation magnitude dominate a layer's output error, while a uniform group scale gives them no more resolution than quiet channels. Measured as: per input channel, mean |x_j| over calibration text beside the column's rounding error; the share of the layer-output error tr(E H Eᵀ) carried by the top channels.
Mechanism: Per input channel of a linear, choose a scale s = mean|x|^α (optionally mixed with weight magnitude, "duo scaling") by grid search over α on the layer output error after quantizing W·diag(s). Multiply s into the consumer's weight columns and divide it out of the producer (RMSNorm gain, or the previous linear's output rows, e.g. v_proj→o_proj, up_proj→down_proj). The transform preserves the function in exact arithmetic, so salient input channels get finer effective resolution at zero added bits. The quantizer that follows can be RTN or GPTQ.
Effect: a salient channel's weight column is multiplied by s_j before rounding, so its effective step becomes Δ(s)/s_j; the quiet channels of the same group get a coarser step; the actual error depends on the group scale and on where each weight falls between levels. The layer-output error at the chosen α is at most the value at the identity scale, which is in the grid; the unquantized function is unchanged.
Preconditions: every smoothed linear has a foldable producer (norm gain or a preceding linear with no nonlinearity in between); fold norms parameterized as (1 + w) into the effective gain, not into w; a mapping table for the architecture exists or can be written (GQA v→o needs head-aware mapping or is skipped); fused serving projections (qkv, gate_up) share one producer, so they share one scale.
Inference overhead: none (scales folded offline).
Pitfalls: the activation statistic is an fp32 sum over tokens, and its value depends on the partition. The same calibration split over several processes gives a slightly different mean|x| than one process. When two grid candidates have close losses, this difference can flip the argmin, and the two builds then differ in their codes, not only in their scales. Accumulation in float64 with a fixed reduction order narrows the difference but does not guarantee the same argmin. Second: a norm written as x*(1+w) has effective gain (1+w); dividing w by s instead of (1+w) leaves a residual scale that is not an identity. This residual is small, passes a loose output check, and moves every reader of that norm.
Origin: Lin et al. (AWQ) observed that keeping a small share of weight channels out of the grid matters only when the channels are chosen by activation magnitude, not weight magnitude. Scaling the salient columns up and folding the inverse into the preceding operator gives the same finer rounding inside a uniform grid.
