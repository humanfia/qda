# long-generation-drift
Observable: the model's behaviour over a long generation moves while its agreement with the source over short contexts does not. Give the source and the candidate the same prompts and let both generate freely to a long limit. Record the distribution of output lengths and the fraction of generations that reach the limit instead of stopping on their own. These two can shift between builds whose position-by-position agreement on a fixed text, over a few hundred tokens, is indistinguishable.
How to see it on your own model: teacher-force a fixed text and record the per-position agreement with the source; this is the short-context reading. Then sample continuations from the same prompts with a fixed decoding configuration and a fixed seed. Record the length distribution and the stop reason. The phenomenon is present when the second reading moves and the first does not. Agreement is measured per position on text the model did not produce. A generation is a composition of its own choices. So a small bias in a stop-or-continue decision compounds over hundreds of steps and is never large at any one step. Length is not a quality measure in either direction. Decoding settings change the length distribution too, so pin them before attributing anything.
Cards: lptq-e2e-scale-tune, obj-gradient-token-weights
