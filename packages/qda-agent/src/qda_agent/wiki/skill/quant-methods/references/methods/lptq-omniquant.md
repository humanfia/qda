# lptq-omniquant
Addresses: the most extreme weights of a group set its range, so a min/max grid spends its step on a few entries; per-layer calibration lets error accumulate block by block. Measured as: per group, absmax/rms; per block, the block-output MSE against the full-precision block.
Mechanism: Block-wise reconstruction. Per decoder block, it trains learnable weight clipping (sigmoid-bounded strengths on the min and max of each group) by gradient descent. The loss is the block-output MSE against the full-precision block, and the weights stay fixed. The paper also learns an equivalent transformation (a channel scale and shift moved between activations and weights). In this weight-only form, only its channel scale enters, and only where it folds into a preceding norm or linear.
Effect: per group, the clipped range shrinks to the learned strength, so the step size falls; the block-output MSE on calibration falls from its initialisation; the stored grid is the target scheme's, and a folded channel scale leaves the unquantized function unchanged.
Preconditions: a channel scale only where a norm gain or a preceding linear sits directly before the reader (q/k/v and gate/up, v_proj before o_proj); clipping alone needs no folding; one block and its cached inputs fit on a card.
Inference overhead: none (clipping changes only scales and zero points; a channel scale is folded offline).
Pitfalls: the shift half folds only into a bias; a projection without a bias cannot absorb it. Adding a bias changes the stored tensors and the kernel, so the weight-only form keeps the shift off. If the trainer keeps the shift and the export drops it, the training loss does not describe the saved model. Second: the learned channel scale is the same degree of freedom as a scale-migration conditioner; with both, the second fits only the residue of the first.
Origin: Shao et al. observed that two extreme weights decide the min/max scale of a group. They also observed that a channel transform fixed by a formula does not see the block output. So they fit only the quantizer parameters, with the pretrained weights fixed, against the full-precision block output.
Source: Shao et al., "OmniQuant: Omnidirectionally Calibrated Quantization for Large Language Models", ICLR 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13137 · code: https://github.com/OpenGVLab/OmniQuant
